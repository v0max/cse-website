Joseph Bonneau researches computer security and applied cryptography, with a focus on decentralized systems and blockchains.

He is an Associate Professor of Computer Science at New York University’s Courant Institute and has previously taught at Stanford, Princeton and the University of Melbourne. His research spans computer security, applied cryptography, decentralized systems, human behavior and theoretical cryptography.

He helped pioneer the academic study of blockchain systems, cryptographic approaches to transparency, and cryptographically verifiable lotteries, and has deep technical expertise in cryptocurrencies, encrypted messaging tools, TLS and web encryption. He is a co-author of the freely available textbook *Bitcoin and Cryptocurrency Technologies*.

He was a Research Partner at Andreessen Horowitz (a16z crypto) from 2022 to 2025, a Technology Fellow at the Electronic Frontier Foundation from 2015 to 2017, and a research scientist at Google from 2012 to 2013.
